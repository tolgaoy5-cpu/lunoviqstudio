HMRC mileage rates 2026/27: 55p a mile explained

The approved mileage rates from 6 April 2026: 55p for cars and vans, 25p after 10,000 miles, 24p for motorcycles and 20p for bicycles, and who can claim them.

Updated 2026-11-05

From 6 April 2026 the HMRC approved mileage rate for cars and vans went up from 45p to 55p a mile for the first 10,000 business miles in the tax year. It is the first change since 2011.

The rates for 2026/27

VehicleFirst 10,000 business milesOver 10,000 miles
Cars and vans55p25p
Motorcycles24p24p
Bicycles (employees)20p20p

Trips before 6 April 2026 still use the old 45p rate. The 10,000 miles are counted across all your cars and vans for the tax year, not per vehicle.

Who can use these rates

  • Employees using their own vehicle: your employer can pay up to these rates tax-free. If they pay less, you can claim tax relief on the difference.
  • Sole traders: you can use the flat rate instead of working out actual running costs. It goes in SA103S box 12 (car, van and travel expenses). You cannot switch to it for a vehicle you have already claimed capital allowances on.
  • Landlords: business journeys for your rental properties, such as inspections or meeting contractors, can be claimed against property income.

What counts as a business trip

Travel to a temporary workplace, a client, a supplier or one of your rental properties. Ordinary commuting from home to a permanent workplace does not count.

What to record for each trip

  • Date
  • Start and end place (HMRC asks employees for postcodes)
  • Purpose of the trip
  • Business miles
  • Vehicle

Example

A sole trader drives 12,000 business miles in 2026/27: 10,000 × 55p = £5,500, plus 2,000 × 25p = £500. The claim is £6,000.

Questions

What is the HMRC mileage rate for 2026/27?

55p a mile for cars and vans for the first 10,000 business miles, then 25p. Motorcycles are 24p and bicycles 20p.

When did the 55p rate start?

It applies to business miles from 6 April 2026. Trips before that date use 45p.

Can landlords claim mileage?

Yes, for journeys made for the rental business, such as property inspections. Ordinary commuting does not count.

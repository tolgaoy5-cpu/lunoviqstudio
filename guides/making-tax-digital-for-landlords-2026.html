Making Tax Digital for landlords: what changed in April 2026

Who must use Making Tax Digital for Income Tax, the £50,000, £30,000 and £20,000 thresholds, the quarterly update deadlines and what records to keep.

Updated 2026-11-06

Making Tax Digital (MTD) for Income Tax started on 6 April 2026. Landlords and sole traders in it keep digital records and send HMRC a short update every quarter, then a final declaration after the tax year.

Who has to join, and when

Qualifying income is your gross rental income plus any self-employment income, before expenses. A landlord with £32,000 of rent and no other self-employment is not in MTD for 2026/27, but will be from April 2027.

Quarterly update deadlines

The quarters follow the tax year. Each update is due by:

  • 7 August for 6 April to 5 July
  • 7 November for 6 July to 5 October
  • 7 February for 6 October to 5 January
  • 7 May for 6 January to 5 April

The final declaration replaces the old Self Assessment return and is due by 31 January after the tax year.

What a quarterly update contains

Totals of your property income and expenses to date, grouped into HMRC's categories (for example rent received, repairs and maintenance, finance costs, legal and professional fees). It is not a tax payment and you do not work out tax each quarter.

What records to keep

  • Every rent receipt and every cost, with the date, amount and category
  • Which property each item belongs to
  • Mortgage interest separately, because it gets the Section 24 tax credit rather than a deduction
  • Capital spending (improvements, furniture bought for the first time) kept apart from repairs

Can I keep using a spreadsheet?

Yes, as long as the figures reach HMRC through MTD-compatible software. Many landlords keep their records in a spreadsheet and send the quarterly totals with bridging software that reads it. A spreadsheet on its own cannot send anything to HMRC.

Questions

Do I need MTD if my rent is under £50,000?

Not in 2026/27, unless your rent plus self-employment income is over £50,000. Check again for April 2027 (£30,000) and April 2028 (£20,000).

Is the threshold before or after expenses?

Before expenses. It is your gross rental income plus gross self-employment income.

When is the first quarterly update due?

For the quarter 6 April to 5 July 2026, the update was due by 7 August 2026. Later updates are due by 7 November, 7 February and 7 May.
